The World's Best Destinations for Fall Foliage

From scenic drives through New England to temple gardens in Kyoto, here are nine destinations to consider for a fall foliage trip.

New England gets plenty of attention during leaf-peeping season, but it’s hardly the only place worth visiting in the fall. Across the globe, Kyoto’s maples turn red around centuries-old temples, larches change to gold in the Dolomites, and cities like Paris and Seoul juxtapose fall color with urban life. Here are nine destinations to consider for a fall foliage trip.

Mont-Tremblant, Quebec, Canada

Peak: Late September to early October

About a 90-minute drive northwest of Montreal, Mont-Tremblant sits in Quebec’s Laurentian Mountains. The drive can be part of the trip: Route 327 winds through the Laurentians, passing Saint-Sauveur, known for its lively village center, and Val-David, an artsy mountain town with galleries and cafés. From late September into October, sugar maples turn deep red and orange, while birch trees add bright yellow to the landscape. In Mont-Tremblant, take the panoramic gondola to the summit for views over the changing forest, or hike in Mont-Tremblant National Park, where trails run through dense woods and past lakes and rivers.

Bavaria, Germany

Peak: Early to mid-October

Fall color spreads all across Bavaria — in its forests, around its lakes, and over its Alpine foothills in the south. In the Allgäu region, changing leaves surround Neuschwanstein Castle, while farther east, the Berchtesgaden region has mountain trails and forests around Lake Königssee. Beyond the foliage, one of fall’s most memorable sights is the Viehscheid in Allgäu, when cattle, often decorated with flowers and bells, return from their summer pastures in the mountains. Some towns mark the occasion with festivities.

The Berkshires, Massachusetts, USA

Peak: Mid-October 

Come October, much of Massachusetts turns red, orange, and gold — and the Berkshires in the western part of the state are particularly popular for leaf-peeping. Route 7 runs through charming towns like Stockbridge, famous for its historic center and Norman Rockwell Museum; farther north, Williamstown is home to the Clark Art Institute and its 140-acre grounds. You can also drive the Mohawk Trail, a 63-mile stretch of Route 2 through the northern Berkshires, passing forested hills, small towns, and scenic overlooks, with a stop at an orchard for fresh apple cider and a cider doughnut. If time allows, detour to Mount Greylock, the state’s highest peak, for views across the surrounding hills.

The Dolomites, Italy

Peak: Mid- to late October

Come fall, the Dolomites’ larch trees turn gold against the pale limestone peaks, with more autumn color in the forests and vineyards below. October is a great time to see the changing larches, particularly around Lago di Braies and Val Gardena. Around sunset, you may also catch enrosadira, when the mountains take on shades of pink and red. Fall also coincides with Törggelen season in the northern Dolomites  — South Tyrol specifically — when you can enjoy newly harvested wine, roasted chestnuts, and regional dishes like canederli (bread dumplings) and Schlutzkrapfen (stuffed pasta) after the grape harvest.

Perthshire, Scotland

Peak: Mid- to late October

Highland Perthshire, a mountainous area in central Scotland, lives up to its nickname, “Big Tree Country,” with its extensive woodlands. In October, fall color spreads through the forests around Pitlochry, a Victorian-era resort town, and Dunkeld, home to Dunkeld Cathedral on the banks of the River Tay. Nearby, trails through The Hermitage pass beneath Douglas firs and other towering trees on the way to Black Linn Waterfall. You can also walk through Faskally Forest and along Loch Faskally, or head to Queen’s View, which overlooks Loch Tummel and the surrounding hills.

Paris, France

Peak: Mid-October to early November

Paris may not be the first place that comes to mind for a foliage trip, but autumn brings plenty of color to the City of Lights. In the Jardin du Luxembourg, rows of trees turn gold around the fountains and palace, while the Tuileries Garden offers colorful views between the Louvre and Place de la Concorde. In the 19th arrondissement, Parc des Buttes-Chaumont has a lake, tree-lined paths, and plenty of fall color across its hilly grounds. The Jardin des Plantes is another fall favorite, particularly for its botanical gardens and variety of trees.
