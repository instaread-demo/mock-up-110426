Florida's Abandoned Amusement Park Is Thriving Again As Lush Gardens With Dinosaur Relics

Florida is known for being full of unexpected treasures. Though its 825 miles of sandy shores and thousands more miles of coastline draw in countless visitors, it's also a place where you can find historic Spanish forts like Castillo de San Marcos and Fort San Marcos de Apalache, alligators and crocodiles roaming in the wild, and even mermaid shows your family will get a kick out of. But just outside Daytona Beach, there's another unexpected treasure: Dunlawton Sugar Mill Gardens, whose secret will take you back millions of years into the past. Or at the very least, back to the 1940s.

Dunlawton Sugar Mill Gardens in Port Orange has quite the storied past. At one point, it was a sugarcane and cotton plantation that switched ownership a few times. Some time after becoming the Dunlawton Sugar Mill (which still used slave labor), it was successfully destroyed by the Seminoles. It also served as a Confederate military base for a time. But by the 1940s, a portion of the area was leased to a dermatologist named Dr. Perry Sperber, who was fascinated with prehistoric creatures. More specifically, Sperber loved dinosaurs. He loved them so much that he decided to build an entire theme park around replicas of the ancient giants.

The park was named Bongoland, and featured several life-sized terrible lizards constructed out of concrete and chicken wire, as well as a recreation of a Seminole village, a miniature train, and some live animals. One of them was a baboon named Bongo. The Jurassic-style park closed to the public just five years after opening. Fortunately, though, the massive dinos have survived the decades since, becoming one with the gardens that thrive there today. Best of all, you can visit them for free anytime.

Dinosaurs at Dunlawton Sugar Mill Gardens

JennLShoots/Shutterstock

Since 1985, the Dunlawton Sugar Mill Gardens has existed on the land where Bongoland once stood. Spread across 10 acres in east central Florida, the volunteer-run gardens are home to numerous plants and flowers, from magnolias and azaleas to ferns, succulents, and, of course, palms. To find the dinos, though, you'll want to head closer to the children's garden, where the life-size creatures lurk. 

Among the dinosaurs you may encounter are a triceratops, a stegosaurus, a dimetrodon, and others. At one point, Bongoland even boasted a Tyrannosaurus that was more than 40 feet tall. Sadly, it was knocked down by a hurricane in 2019, but visitors can still see remnants of the statue today. Dino enthusiasts will delight in finding them all and snapping photos along the way.

Outside of the dinos and plants, the gardens also feature multiple walking paths that will take you to other charming points of interest. There's also a tiny gnome village, complete with small statues of wee magical beings, animals, mushrooms, and miniature houses. Past visitors also note the cute stone walking bridge and some peaceful ponds where you'll see other colorful trinkets adding an extra bit of whimsy to the area. You can also see what's left of the old historic sugar mill here. Mostly, though, you'll enjoy having a shady place to enjoy Florida's great outdoors — with a side of dino, of course.

Extending your visit outside of Bongoland

JennLShoots/Shutterstock

While a worthy stop for any dino-fiend, the gardens formerly known as Bongoland likely won't take up all of your time in the Port Orange area. You can easily include it as a longer trip to the town, or as part of a road trip stop across the Sunshine State.

There are plenty of nearby accommodations to choose from during your visit to Port Orange. Inland, there are a few budget-friendly hotel chains like La Quinta and Country Inn & Suites by Radisson that offer rooms starting at around $85 a night (at the time of writing). Or if you'd prefer to stay beachside, there are plenty of options all along nearby Daytona Shores, ranging from $100-a-night options like Beachside Hotel to slightly more expensive options like the Hilton Vacation Club Daytona Seabreeze Daytona Beach.

Florida's weather is typically warm, humid, and a little bit buggy, so make sure to pack sunscreen and bug spray for your visit. As for what else to do in the area, New Smyrna Beach, considered to be one of the East Coast beaches that are a must-visit for surfers, is less than 20 minutes south of the town. And about an hour north, you can also experience American history with Spanish flair in St. Augustine.

Florida's Stunning Gulf Coast Beach Feels Like Visiting The Bahamas

By Felix Gott Aug. 25, 2026 6:30 am EST
Artiom Photo/Shutterstock

The Bahamas is one of the most spectacular places on Earth for anyone looking for a paradise beach getaway or searching for a dose of winter sunshine. With some of the clearest water on the planet, stunning beaches, amazing reefs and marine life, and a selection of elegant luxury resorts, it is no surprise that Americans flock there for their vacations.

But while there's no denying the appeal of the Bahamas, Americans are lucky enough to have similar island vibes much closer to home. Florida's Gulf Coast is home to a stunning beach that feels just like a visit to the Bahamas, with the same crystal-clear turquoise water and sugar-soft white-sand beaches. St. Pete Beach, near Tampa, is one of the most spectacular beaches in Florida, and the perfect option for a sun-drenched escape without leaving the country.

St. Pete Beach is on a barrier island on Florida's Gulf Coast, just to the west of St. Petersburg and Tampa Bay, and north of Shell Key and the stunning Fort de Soto coastal park. It is roughly a 20 minute drive to St. Petersburg, and 45 minutes to Clearwater and Tampa. The nearest international airport to St. Pete Beach is St. Pete–Clearwater International Airport (PIE), although many travelers choose to arrive at Tampa International Airport (TPA), which is slightly further away but is served by a wider variety of airlines and routes.

Miles of beaches fringed by warm turquoise waters

Mohannad Khatib/Shutterstock

It is easy to see why St. Pete Beach and its surrounding areas are routinely compared to the Bahamas and other, more exotic tropical destinations. Miles and miles of coastline offer up a stunning array of award-winning beaches, from Treasure Island in the north to Pass-a-Grille in the south. Fringed by charming beach bars and delightful casual eateries, it is the ideal spot to sit, watch the waves, catch some rays, and enjoy a dip in the warm waters of the Gulf. The views are special, and never more so than at sunset, when the sun dives beneath the horizon right in front of the beach.

Those who have been have high praise for St. Pete Beach. Over on TikTok, @thetacohearter went viral with a short video of the pristine water where they noted, "It literally looks like the Bahamas. What the h***, it's just so perfect." Meanwhile, over on Reddit, u/Dk10c responded to a question about which area beaches to visit by saying, "To answer your questions directly, St. Pete Beach is a great base, especially for access to downtown St. Pete. Yes, it's as beautiful as people say."

St. Pete Beach is hardly a hidden gem. It was voted Tripadvisor's best U.S. beach in 2021, and regularly appears in lists of the best beaches in the country and even around the world. But it still retains a certain laid-back, island charm, in keeping with its Bahamian comparisons. It also means you can avoid the massive crowds that often overwhelm nearby spots like Clearwater Beach.

What to do in St. Pete Beach

Brandon Moser/Getty Images

For many people, just lazing back on the sand and watching the world go by is all the entertainment they need on a beach vacation. And if that's the vibe you are looking for, then St. Pete Beach will deliver. But if you want something more, there's plenty on offer as well. Start with a stroll along the beach to check out the iconic and historic Don CeSar Hotel. This magnificent, cotton-candy pink beauty has been one of St. Pete's most famous landmarks since 1928, and is a sight to behold.

If you're looking for something a bit more active, the emerald-turquoise waters of the Gulf aren't just great for swimming. St. Pete Beach is a wonderful spot for watersports, from windsurfing and parasailing to sport fishing and paddleboarding. It's also a great place for wildlife lovers, with dolphin pods regularly seen offshore, both from the pier and by dolphin boat tour.

Another great option is to hire a bike and cycle south along Gulf Boulevard to check out historic Pass-a-Grille at the southern tip of the barrier island. With its charming old-timey 8th Avenue, it's a delightful way to spend an afternoon, and there are often an array of interesting and unusual cultural events going on, like the Volkswagen Beach Bash in October.
